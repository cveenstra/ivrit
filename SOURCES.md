# Sources

Everything in this course was checked against a source; anything that could not be confirmed was left out. Course data is adapted from English Wiktionary and English Wikipedia under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Pages were fetched with `action=render` in October 2026 and cached by `scripts/wikt.py`; `scripts/build_data.py` rebuilds every item from that cache.

## Words

680 words and word forms in 23 units and 177 lessons.

For each word, `build_data.py` looks up its English Wiktionary page and keeps it only if a Hebrew headword there has a sense matching the course's English gloss. Each comma or semicolon part of the gloss is checked against the senses on its own, and any part not found is dropped (text in parentheses is explanation, not checked). From that headword it takes:

- the pointed spelling (with niqqud) and the unpointed spelling Israelis write (ktiv male), which can add a ו or י;
- the transliteration, converted to the modern form where Wiktionary gives a scholarly one as well;
- the gender, for nouns.

Present-tense verb forms come from the conjugation table on each verb's Wiktionary page (for צָרִיךְ "need", which Wiktionary lists as an adjective, from its headword line). Feminine adjectives come from the adjective's headword line. The feminine counting numbers (שָׁלוֹשׁ, חָמֵשׁ…) and זֹאת are listed on Wiktionary only as "feminine of" the masculine word; they are kept because the masculine word is itself a checked item.

### Transliteration of inflected forms

Most inflected forms have no Wiktionary page of their own, so their transliteration is generated by rule from the pointed spelling (`scripts/translit.py`): 144 of 680 items, marked `tr_src: "rule"` in `data/items.json`. Run on the 507 course words that do have a Wiktionary transliteration, the same rules agree with Wiktionary on 503 (99.2%), ignoring accent marks and apostrophes. The ones that differ:

- חֵצִי: Wiktionary *khatsí*, rule *khetsi*
- בֵּית־מִרְקַחַת: Wiktionary *béit-mirkákhat*, rule *betmirkakhat*
- טִיּוּל: Wiktionary *tiyúl*, rule *tiul*
- אֵיזֶה: Wiktionary *éize*, rule *eze*

Stress (the accent mark) on generated forms follows the present-tense and adjective pattern: last syllable, except feminine singular forms ending in -et/-at, stressed one syllable earlier (*okhélet*). On the 69 inflected forms whose transliteration does come from Wiktionary, this rule picks the same stressed syllable for 61.

## Letters and vowel points

31 letter forms (22 letters, 5 final forms, and the dot / no-dot pairs בּ ב, כּ כ, פּ פ, שׁ שׂ) and 14 vowel points. `scripts/build_letters.py` reads names, Hebrew names and modern pronunciation from the tables in Wikipedia's [Hebrew alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet) article (the letter table and the Israeli standard transliteration table) and the Israeli rows of the vowel table in [Niqqud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niqqud), and fails if they disagree with what the course teaches. Example words for each letter are the ones in the Israeli transliteration table. ח is taught as *kh*: the Academy of the Hebrew Language writes it *ẖ*, but it sounds the same as כ today. ג׳ ז׳ צ׳ (loanword sounds) are not taught.

Syllable and word readings in the Letters course are built by rule from each letter's and vowel's sound, to show how the script works; real words in those lessons are course words written only with letters already learned.

## Tracing

27 stroke guides in `data/tracing.json`, drawn by hand for this app as simplified block-print shapes. They follow common conventions for writing print Hebrew by hand (top bars first and left to right, uprights top to bottom, bases last), but there is no single official stroke order and they are **not** taken from a dataset. Cursive (ktav yad) is not included: a guide that teaches it well would need a proper source, and a rough one would teach the wrong shapes.

## Audio

The app records no audio. 🔊 buttons and listening questions use the browser's own Hebrew text-to-speech voice when the device has one, and are hidden otherwise.
